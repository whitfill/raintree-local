**Post 2 — Proof of work (Boga case study)**

A rug importer came to me with 1,079 products on their website.

Every single one had either no SEO metadata or metadata that was auto-generated garbage — titles like "Product 3847" and descriptions that said "Beautiful rug. Buy now."

Google had indexed almost none of them.

In week one, we identified all 1,079 products, built a template for titles, descriptions, and keywords based on the actual terminology buyers use, and bulk-updated the entire catalog.

In week two, we found 228 broken URLs — pages that used to exist, had real backlinks pointing to them, and were now returning 404 errors. We mapped every one to the right current page and implemented redirects.

In week three, we built out 10 neighborhood pages targeting the specific search queries their customers were using: "Persian rugs Houston," "vintage rugs Austin," "oriental rugs Dallas."

By week four, impressions in Google Search Console were up. Real traffic was starting to move.

The work isn't glamorous. It's audit, prioritize, fix, measure, repeat.

But it compounds. And six months from now, every one of those 1,079 products has a chance to rank for something a buyer is actually searching for.

That's what local SEO looks like in practice.

**Post 3 — Education (no pitch)**

Most small business owners think SEO is a one-time project.

Do the work once. Rank forever.

Here's what actually happens:

Google's algorithms change. Competitors add pages. Your own site grows and new broken links appear. Product pages get created with no metadata. Blog posts go stale.

SEO isn't a project. It's a system.

The businesses that consistently win in local search aren't the ones who did the most aggressive campaign in 2022.

They're the ones who have been quietly doing the boring fundamentals every month:

— Fix broken pages when they appear
— Add real descriptions to new products
— Post on Google Business Profile weekly
— Respond to reviews within 24 hours
— Add one piece of genuinely useful content per month

None of that is exciting.

All of it compounds.

If you're a small local business thinking about SEO, stop thinking about it as a campaign and start thinking about it as maintenance — like keeping your physical space clean and organized.

The businesses that show up first in Google aren't usually smarter. They're more consistent.
